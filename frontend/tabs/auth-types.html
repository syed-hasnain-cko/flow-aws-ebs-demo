⚠ Prerequisite: Partial authorization only works in sandbox if Checkout.com's account team has enabled it for your account.

How to tell which outcome you got — check these three fields in the response (shown below the Balances table after you pay):
  • response_code: "10010" = partial approval worked. "10000" = feature is not enabled on your account — you got a normal full approval instead.
  • response_summary: should read "Partial Value Approved", not "Approved".
  • amount vs amount_requested: on a real partial approval, amount (e.g. 5000) is lower than amount_requested (e.g. 10000) — if they're equal, it's a full approval.
If you're seeing response_code: "10000" with amount equal to what you sent, partial authorization isn't enabled for this sandbox account yet — contact your account manager, this isn't something fixable client-side.

Use one of the test cards below with amount exactly 10000 or 1000 (minor units) — any other amount just processes as a normal payment regardless of whether the feature is enabled.
Set authorization_type: "Estimated". Automatic capture is disabled by Checkout.com for estimated authorizations regardless of the Capture toggle. Once approved, you can call Increment Authorization (Section 5) any number of times before capturing — to increase the amount, extend the validity period, or both, depending on scheme support shown there.
Required for US internet gambling merchants (MCC 7801); optional but recommended for everyone else.

How to verify: Checkout.com's docs define no sandbox simulation or response flag for this feature — it's silent risk-data enrichment sent to the issuer. There are only two things you can actually check:
  • The payment still Authorized — proves the extra fields didn't break the request (a typo'd field would 422 instead).
  • Every highlighted field below was actually sent — see the "Enhanced Data Fields Sent" checklist that appears after you pay.
Its real effect (fewer false declines, 60-100bps approval uplift) is only visible statistically over production volume in the Dashboard's Authorization Analytics — not from a single sandbox call.
